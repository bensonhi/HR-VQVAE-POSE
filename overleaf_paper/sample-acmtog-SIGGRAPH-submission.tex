\begin{abstract}
Believable digital humans require co-speech gestures that are temporally
aligned with speech, semantically meaningful, and smoothly coordinated
across face, body, and hands---yet existing methods fall short on
long-form coherence and generalization. Discrete motion codebooks
introduce artifacts in continuous pose space, lack explicit mechanisms
for kinematic continuity, and are typically evaluated on single
speakers. We present GestureCraft, a planning-and-generation framework
for synthesizing full-body SMPL-X gestures directly from speech. Our
model centers on a body-part-hierarchical Transformer VAE with a
continuous latent space partitioned into face, body, and hand
slices, each supervised by partial-output decoding losses on its
assigned body part. A conditional
flow-matching prior generates this latent conditioned on audio, speaker
identity, and gesture type. To support long-form generation, we
introduce anchor-prefix conditioning with a continuation velocity loss
that enforces smooth transitions across chunk boundaries. A fine-tuned
large language model plans gesture-type segments from transcripts,
eliminating the need for dataset-specific annotations at inference.
Trained on multi-speaker BEAT2, GestureCraft achieves state-of-the-art
performance and demonstrates cross-speaker generalization under the
standard evaluation protocol.
\end{abstract}
